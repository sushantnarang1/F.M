More Than a Century of Freemasonry in Kearny

From Copestone Lodge No. 147 to today's Copestone-Ophir Lodge No. 108, the story is held in records, buildings, family memories, and the people who carried it forward.

RECORDS / MEMORY / RESEARCH

The lodge's history is not a single date. It is a continuing story—with documented landmarks, personal acts of care, and questions still waiting for evidence.

We distinguish documented facts from historical interpretation and unresolved questions. If you can help strengthen the record, we would be grateful to hear from you.

01 / THE TIMELINE

Milestones & open questions

A record still
unfolding.

Dates below reflect the historical research shared with the lodge. The exact charter and consolidation dates remain unconfirmed.

  1. Before 1900

    Copestone Lodge No. 147

    Documentary evidence establishes Copestone Lodge No. 147 as existing in Kearny before 1900. Its exact original charter date remains to be confirmed.

    Documented existence · Charter date unresolved
  2. 1900

    Named in county history

    A history of Hudson and Bergen Counties identifies Copestone Lodge No. 147 members, including men who had served as Worshipful Master.

    Genealogical history, Hudson & Bergen Counties
  3. 1916

    Copestone Temple is built

    New Jersey historic-preservation documentation dates the construction of Copestone Temple to 1916. This documented building date is distinct from the lodge's earlier history.

  4. 1910s–1920s

    A place in Kearny life

    The Temple formed part of Kearny's commercial and social landscape along Kearny Avenue.

    Community context
  5. June 11, 1922

    Brotherly Love & Relief

    When Brother Charles “Charlie” Green became ill with tuberculosis, fellow Copestone Masons helped him travel to Saranac Lake, New York, for treatment. They bought his ticket and gave him $100 for the journey. A fellow member later sent $150 for winter clothing. Green arrived June 11, 1922, and eventually recovered.

    A documented act of care
  6. June 1941

    A federal exemption record

    The surviving federal fraternal tax-exemption record dates from June 1941. It is not the founding date of Copestone Lodge or Copestone-Ophir Lodge.

    Important distinction
  7. World War II era

    Members in service

    Members of Copestone served in the United States Armed Forces, including Brother William Heberer in the Army Air Corps.

  8. 1953

    Highland dance at the Temple

    Mary Stewart began teaching Scottish Highland dancing at the Kearny Masonic Temple, connecting the building with the area's Scottish-American community.

  9. 1955

    Robert “Bob” Jones

    Jones received the Entered Apprentice degree on April 27, the Fellow Craft degree on May 25, and was raised a Master Mason on June 22.

  10. 1974–1975

    A gift across the Atlantic

    During the 1974–75 Masonic year, Copestone Lodge No. 147 sent a framed Masonic gift to Lodge Kil Bryd No. 1667 in Scotland.

    Scottish connection
  11. 1975

    Jones serves as Worshipful Master

    Robert Jones later served as Worshipful Master in 1975.

  12. Exact date unknown

    Two lodges, one continuing story

    Available evidence indicates a consolidation involving Copestone Lodge No. 147 and Ophir Lodge No. 108. The consolidated lodge ultimately became Copestone-Ophir Lodge No. 108. The date has not been confirmed from Grand Lodge proceedings.

    Unresolved question · No date inferred
  13. 2011

    Burns Night documented

    A Burns Night gathering is documented as part of the lodge's Scottish heritage.

  14. 2012

    Honoring veterans

    Copestone-Ophir held a ceremony honoring veteran brethren with military-Masonic pins.

  15. 2014–2015

    Sean H. McNorton

    Sean H. McNorton served as Worshipful Master in 2014 and 2015.

  16. March 10, 2024

    In the community

    Lodge members participated publicly in the United Irish St. Patrick's Day Parade.

  17. Present day

    Continuing at 225 Kearny Avenue

    Copestone-Ophir Lodge No. 108 remains associated with 225 Kearny Avenue, Kearny, New Jersey 07032. Its members continue the work and care for the historical record.

02 / THE BUILDING

Kearny Avenue · 1916

The Copestone Temple — Built 1916

New Jersey historic-preservation documentation identifies the building as the Copestone Temple and dates its construction to 1916. Its Neoclassical design is described through its architectural details and Masonic inscriptions.

  • Neoclassical architecture
  • Double-height Corinthian pilasters
  • Classical denticulated cornice
  • Festoon ornamentation and pediment
  • Square and Compasses
  • Historic “COPESTONE TEMPLE” lettering

The uploaded photographs do not include a verified exterior image of the Temple. No building image has been substituted or recreated.

03 / BROTHERLY LOVE & RELIEF

A story of practical care

Help for a brother
on the journey.

When Charles “Charlie” Green fell ill with tuberculosis, his fellow Copestone Masons gathered money to help him reach treatment in Saranac Lake. They purchased his ticket and gave him $100 for his journey. Another member later sent $150 so he could buy winter clothing. Green arrived June 11, 1922, and eventually recovered.

This account offers a concrete example of the principles of Brotherly Love, Relief, and Truth expressed through members' care for one another.

SARANAC LAKE, NEW YORK · 1922
04 / PEOPLE & CULTURE

Scottish-American Kearny

Heritage carried
in community.

Scottish immigrants among the lodge's members helped shape connections beyond the lodge room. Beginning in 1953, Mary Stewart taught Highland dancing at the Masonic Temple. The lodge's documented community ties include John “Jock” Nisbet, the Scots American Club, the Kearny Caledonian Pipe Band, Argyle Fish and Chip Shop, and The Pipers Cove.

Burns Night became an important lodge tradition; a gathering is documented in 2011. The relationship reached across the Atlantic as well: during 1974–75, Copestone Lodge No. 147 sent a framed Masonic gift to Lodge Kil Bryd No. 1667 in Scotland.

These connections are presented as historical research supplied to the lodge; individual relationships and dates should be checked against original records as they become available.

05 / COUNTRY & CRAFT

In service to country

Remembering
those who served.

The lodge's historical record includes members who served in the United States Armed Forces. Documented examples include RW John A. Driscoll, United States Marine Corps, and Brother William Heberer, United States Army Air Corps, during World War II.

In 2012, Copestone-Ophir honored veteran brethren in a ceremony with military-Masonic pins—a public acknowledgment of service to Country and Craft.

06 / THE OPEN QUESTION

Two Lodges, One Continuing Story

Copestone Lodge No. 147→Copestone-Ophir Lodge No. 108

Historical evidence points to a consolidation involving Copestone No. 147 and Ophir No. 108, with the resulting lodge ultimately retaining No. 108. The exact consolidation date has not yet been confirmed in Grand Lodge proceedings. We will not fill that gap with speculation.
